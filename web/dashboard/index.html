GPS apps freeze the moment you actually need them

Drive into a tunnel, an underground car park, or a street between tall buildings, and your phone loses satellite signal. Google Maps freezes, jumps to the wrong spot, or gives you the wrong turn — right when you can't afford it. Emergency vehicles, delivery riders, and everyday drivers all hit this same wall, every single day.

ISRO asked for an AI system that solves this using only a smartphone — no connection to the vehicle, no extra hardware, just the accelerometer and gyroscope already inside every phone.

Turn the phone's motion sensors into a backup navigator

DrishtiNav watches how the phone physically moves — every tiny wobble, turn, and bump — and an AI model trained on real driving data turns that into a live guess of the car's speed, replacing the speedometer it doesn't have access to.

A physics-based fusion engine blends that AI guess with GPS whenever GPS is available, and snaps the result back onto the real road using an offline map the moment GPS disappears. The "you are here" dot never freezes. It never jumps. It just keeps going.

Every requirement ISRO asked for, ticked off

Click any card to see exactly how we proved it. Nothing here is a claim without a receipt.

✓Train & test on real driving data (IO-VNBD)▾
We loaded the official IO-VNBD dataset — and found 5 real bugs in it first (recordings out of sync by up to 26 seconds, sensor columns swapped, clocks that reset mid-recording). We fixed all of them before training on 330 km of real driving. Full writeup →
✓AI predicts vehicle speed — no speedometer needed▾
SpeedNet, an 18,787-parameter neural network trained on real driving, predicts forward speed and how confident it is from 20 seconds of phone motion data alone. Test accuracy: 2.53 m/s (~9 km/h) RMSE. See the numbers →
✓Filters engine vibration, potholes & phone-mount misalignment▾
The app automatically learns exactly how your phone is angled in its holder — and notices if it gets bumped or re-seated mid-drive and re-learns on the fly. Road bumps and engine vibration are filtered out before anything reaches the AI. View the code →
✓Physics rules baked in (a car can't slide sideways)▾
The "non-holonomic constraint" — cars move forward/backward and turn, but don't slide sideways or fly — isn't a bolted-on rule, it's built directly into the core motion model that drives every prediction.
✓AI-based GPS + motion sensor fusion▾
The fusion engine doesn't just average GPS and AI — it trusts the AI's speed guess exactly as much as the AI itself says it should be trusted, sample by sample. When the AI is unsure, GPS (or the road map) gets more say. Technical detail →
✓Map matching on an offline map — no internet needed▾
Real OpenStreetMap road data, downloaded once and stored locally. A Hidden Markov Model snaps the estimated position onto the most likely real road, live, with zero internet calls at run time. View the code →
✓Seamless switching — no freezing, no jumping▾
GPS lost → dead-reckoning mode happens in the very same instant. We wrote an automated test that proves the position output never jumps during the switch — it's mathematically continuous, not a restart.
✓Edge-deployable — works on any IMU hardware▾
One codebase runs at ~10 Hz on an ordinary phone and at ~200 Hz on industrial/FOG-grade sensors — both profiles are built, tested and benchmarked below. Run it on your own hardware →
✓Trained models + offline maps ready for demo day▾
No training required at the venue — the trained AI model and every offline road map are already committed in the repository, ready to run instantly.
◐Keep position drift under 10% during a GPS blackout▾
The typical GPS blackout passes this target comfortably — see the live numbers on the Live Demo page. A handful of the hardest test cases don't quite make it yet, and we show you exactly which ones, honestly, instead of hiding them. Every number, including where we fall short →

SpeedNet (AI pseudo-odometer)

How to read this

  • INS + NHC + ZUPT: classical vehicle EKF — phone gyro heading, accelerometer speed, no AI, no map. This is the "basic physics" baseline.
  • + AI speed: SpeedNet predicts forward speed & its own uncertainty from 20 s of IMU; the EKF uses that σ as measurement noise; its scale self-calibrates against GNSS before the outage.
  • + HMM map matching: offline OpenStreetMap; confident matches constrain cross-track position and heading, turns at junctions reset along-track error. This is DrishtiNav's full system.
  • GNSS aiding = reference receiver degraded to 1 Hz smartphone quality; outages injected every L + 90 s.
